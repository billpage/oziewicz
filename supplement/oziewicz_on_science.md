# Oziewicz on the practice of science

> What Zbigniew Oziewicz said, in print and in his 2009 talk, about how science
> should be done: where each of his slogans can be found, what he argued for
> them, and where they sit uneasily with each other and with this repository's
> method. Written 3 November 2026 for the In memoriam section of the README.

## 2. The slogans and where they are

| Slogan | Where |
|---|---|
| Science is subjective | Spoken in the introduction to the 2009 talk (position 1). In print: "My thesis is that a science is and must be subjective" (2013 *Studium Vilnense*, §2.2), and §2.1 defines science as a collection of alternative, subjective and personal ideas. |
| Science must never be objective; objective science is dead science | Title and abstract of both 2013 papers; §2.2 of *Studium Vilnense*. |
| Everybody has a right to be right | Abstract of both 2013 papers, and §2.2 of *Studium Vilnense*. He puts it as the condition for science to stay "a bundle of subjective ideas". |
| Science should be based on dissent | §2 of *Studium Vilnense*, after the "Three times yes" clarification. The same paragraph says that as science becomes publicly funded it becomes dogmatic, that textbooks give one final truth, and that an anonymous referee system bars alternatives. His own wording; the passage also appears in an unpublished draft. |
| Only a controversial subject is interesting | Spoken, 2009 talk, positions 1 and 2: the editor of *Foundations of Physics*, the joke about renaming the journal, and "boring" for the non-controversial. In print the nearest statements are that consensus is "not interesting" and that a subject everybody agrees on is not a subject of science (2013 *Studium Vilnense*, §2 and §4). |
| Dissident | Spoken, 2009 talk, position 5: he calls his group of relativity critics dissidents and a colleague a "second-order dissident". "Dissident squared" at position 6 is a chat participant's phrase that he reads out. "Dissident physics" is not his phrase in anything read so far. |
| Karl Popper was wrong about the nature of science | 2013 *Studium Vilnense*, §1 ("Karl R. Popper: science is objective") and the Conclusion. See §3. |
| Imagination before experiment | Not as a slogan. In substance, §1 of *Studium Vilnense*: free thought "unbiased by experiments" is a precondition of science, the best an experiment can do is stimulate imagination, and "Science must not cringe before experimental data". |
| Other chosen epigraphs (not his own words) | "Science is the meaning of reality, not the photograph of reality" (H. Bergman 1929, in both oziewicz2006boostlink and §2 of *Studium Vilnense*); Einstein 1933 on axioms being freely invented; Poincaré on contradictory theories both being useful; Aleksander Fredro on consensus, which he reverses for science. |

## 3. What he argued against Popper (a paraphrase of §1)

- He credits Popper with rightly refusing experimental *confirmation* as proof of
  a theory.
- He objects that Popper (a) subordinates theoretical imagination to empirical
  engineering, (b) lets technology and laboratories set limits on freedom of
  thought, (c) rests on the "crucial experiment", which he calls a myth after
  Lakatos, and (d) makes a distinction between confirmation and refutation that
  he calls almost irrelevant, while admitting that refutation is more scientific
  than confirmation.
- His Conclusion: there is no criterion that separates science from
  pseudo-science, and the demarcation is a myth serving the guardians of the
  status quo. Science, for him, needs no criteria to decide who is right.

## 4. Where it gets difficult

1. **2009 and 2013 differ on refutation.** In the 2009 talk (position 48) he says
   he would welcome an experiment showing his groupoid theory wrong, and at
   position 51 he works out how hard that test is. In 2013 he rejects refutation
   by experiment as the judge of science, while conceding that it is "more
   scientific" than confirmation. These can be reconciled (a theory may welcome a
   test without any test being a verdict), but he does not reconcile them, and a
   note in this repository should not attribute either position to him alone.
2. **"Subjective" is used with technical content (interpretation).** In an
   unpublished draft he glosses Galileo's "relative" as "subjective, not
   objective" for the place of an event, which depends on the choice of reference
   body. In the mathematics his subjective choices are the observer, the
   reference body or the aether, and what changes when they change is stated
   exactly. The 2013 papers use the word for scientific opinion, in a wider sense.
   Whether the two uses are one thought is for Bill Page to say.
3. **Statements of fact in the 2013 paper that this repository does not adopt.**
   *Studium Vilnense* §1 calls Eddington's 1919 eclipse data a hoax and says the
   CERN neutrino-speed measurement shows that the experiment, not the theory,
   may be at fault. To the best of Claude's knowledge, the 1919 data have been
   re-examined and the choice Eddington made among the plates is generally held
   to be defensible (D. Kennefick, *Physics Today* 62 (3) 2009, "Testing
   relativity from the 1919 eclipse: a question of bias"), and the OPERA neutrino
   anomaly was traced in 2012 to faulty equipment, which an experiment found
   (for example Phys.org, 22 Feb 2012, "Faster than light neutrinos? More like
   faulty wiring"). None of these four was opened in this session; the titles
   come from search results. Check before relying on them.
4. **This repository checks mathematics, not allegiance.** It asks whether an
   identity holds, whether a construction composes, and whether a statement
   follows from its premises, and it says what is checked and how (computer
   algebra or numerical). It takes no position on which theory nature chooses,
   and it does not presume that a claim is wrong for being unorthodox or right
   for being unorthodox. Whether Oziewicz would accept that is open. His
   own papers assert mathematical results as true or false, for example that a
   postulate contradicts relativity of simultaneity, so he evidently did not hold
   mathematical correctness to be a matter of opinion.
