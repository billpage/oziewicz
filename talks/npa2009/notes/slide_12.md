And now let's come to what you can find in every textbook. What is velocity? This is the main problem. What is relative velocity? Einstein wrote: "Let v be the relative velocity of reference system B with respect to A." This is very easy to write! Every child can write that the letter v is a velocity. So here you have the Lorentz transformation, and I am asking: what is v? Do you think that v is just the derivative of x with respect to time? Do you see? Do you believe? Where is my best friend, Harry? Do you believe this, that this velocity is this? What is velocity in these expressions?

Urbantke in 2003 wrote the solution. If you understand what t is, what x is, what x-prime is, what t-prime is, then these are two equations, a system of two equations for the unknown gamma and the unknown v. You multiply the first equation, scalar multiplication, by x; then you insert v dot x from the first equation into the second; then you can eliminate gamma, calculate gamma, put gamma upstairs. When you have gamma in terms of x, x-prime, t and t-prime, you can calculate v from this equation, trivially.
