Therefore, after Minkowski, nobody defines Einstein's special relativity in terms of Einstein's two axioms. Because from Einstein's two axioms, one axiom that the velocity of light is independent of the source, emitter-free, and the other that all reference systems are on an equal footing, you cannot derive the Lorentz transformation. This is impossible. […] In 1905 Einstein postulated two postulates, and most people today think that these postulates really imply the Lorentz transformation. This is wrong. The Lorentz transformation follows from the reciprocity of velocity, from the assumption that the relative velocity of A with respect to B is minus the relative velocity of B with respect to A. From this assumption you get exactly the Lorentz transformation. The velocity of light is totally irrelevant for this consequence; not important. So Schrödinger, Bergmann, Bargmann define special relativity by only one postulate. This postulate was made by Minkowski in 1908: that every pair of physical inertial reference systems is connected by an isometric Lorentz transformation, a transformation that must leave the metric invariant, and that it must be a group. It was supposed that it must be a group, the symmetry group of the metric. No, OK.
