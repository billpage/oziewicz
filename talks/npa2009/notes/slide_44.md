OK. Here you see explicitly how the relative velocity between A and B depends on the choice of aether. And you see that for different choices of aether you get totally different velocities, directions and lengths, between two reference systems. This is the essence of Einstein's relativity: in his special relativity there does not exist a unique velocity between two reference systems. So if you consider the twin paradox, you say that this is the velocity of one twin with respect to the other twin. But which velocity? Einstein's theory of relativity does not have one velocity. It has infinitely many velocities, one for each choice of aether. This is the story. I think that this is very important, but nobody pays attention to it.
