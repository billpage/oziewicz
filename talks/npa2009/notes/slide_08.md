In 1988 Ungar checked, and discovered that if we have four bodies, so three relative velocities u, v and w, and you first compose u with v and then with w, or first compose v with w and then with u, you get totally different results. This was not expected. If Einstein had known this before he died, the situation would be totally different. We have concrete velocities between two reference systems, between this and this, and between this and this, yet Einstein's addition formula gives David two different velocities with respect to Alice, with different directions and different lengths. This is physically very hard to accept. It was discovered in 1988. I learned about it at a conference on non-associative algebras in 1993. I was thinking that probably the addition I had invented before was also non-associative, and I was not so enthusiastic to come back to it. But in 2003 I was lecturing on electromagnetism, and I checked: my formula was associative. So then I came back to special relativity.

About this non-associativity, I am interested in what you think. Do you know this? If you have more bodies and more and more additions, you get as many velocities of David with respect to Alice as you want. You can get an arbitrary velocity of David with respect to Alice in Einstein's special relativity. You know that in 1926 Thomas discovered the Thomas rotation; he was a student of Born. Born wrote a letter to Einstein, and Einstein said this is stupidity: how could there be such a big Thomas rotation for very small velocities? This is somehow strange. But finally they accepted it. But this non-associativity, that between two fixed reference systems you can calculate as many relative velocities as you want, looks like nonsense.

I talked about this with Yuval Ne'eman in Israel, and he said to me, after we talked during breakfast, that if this is true, special relativity must be immediately rejected: it is impossible that between two reference systems we have so many different velocities. But mathematicians were very happy with this discovery of Ungar's. Ungar is in North Dakota, and in 2001 he wrote a monograph about it. Mainstream physicists do not know this, of course. And even if they knew, they would be happy, because they consider that nothing could be wrong with Einstein's special relativity. I would like to ask you, you are now how many, 12 persons: what do you think about such a non-associativity property?
