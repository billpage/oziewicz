Another point was Poincaré, around 1895. He took part in the prize of the King of Sweden for solving the three-body problem. You know that the two-body problem is totally integrable, we know all the integrals, but the three-body system, Sun, Earth and Moon, was not known. He took part in this. It was a terrible calculation, because you must solve a system of second-order differential equations for three bodies with Newton's gravity. He claimed that he had found the solution for three bodies and sent it to the commission (I think it was Möbius). They printed his paper, and the journal was sent to the post office. Then Poincaré recognized during the night that he had made a mistake, that the three-body problem cannot be solved. He asked them to destroy the whole printing of the journal. He discovered that the three-body problem cannot be solved.

But when Poincaré was working out how to solve differential equations, he came to the idea that coordinates are irrelevant. You change, change, change coordinates to find integrals of the motion, and finally come back, again and again; you can see how the Kepler problem in the Newtonian potential is solved in any classical mechanics book. You only change coordinates. So he asked: what are we really doing? We are changing, but what is inside that has intrinsic meaning? And he discovered that it is the state of the three-body system, or of the two-body system: the manifold of states. What we change is the description of the state. We do not change the states. And then finally we find how the state evolves in time.
