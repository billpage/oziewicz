Thank you to Greg Volk for inviting me. I was not sure what subject I should talk about. I am from Poland: born in Poland, educated in Russia, at Saint Petersburg University. Yuri Keilman is my colleague from the same university; we were educated at the same university. I was educated in mathematical physics, so I am rather a mathematician, not a physicist. From my education in mathematics I was always interested in the formulation of physical theories without any convention, without coordinates, in terms of invariant concepts.

Is everybody hearing me OK? OK.

At the end of the 1970s the idea came to me to formulate special relativity without coordinates. What is the Lorentz transformation? Everybody knows, of course. But how do we formulate the relative velocity between two reference frames without coordinates? And how do we understand what a reference frame is without coordinates? After I formulated this, it happened to be exactly the formulation of Minkowski in 1908. From this definition of reference system (I use "reference system" because my reference frame is not a basis) and the definition of relative velocity, I derived a formula for the composition of velocities. But this formula does not coincide with what we have in all the textbooks on special relativity, Einstein's relativity. I did not understand why. Only ten years later, in 2003, did I finally understand why: what the problem is with special relativity as Einstein defined it.
