Now to today's example: From Grassmann to Clifford, which Oziewicz contributed to the volume Clifford Algebras and Their Applications in Mathematical Physics, edited by Chisholm and Common and published by Reidel in 1986.

The thesis of the paper, as I read it, has three claims.

First: exterior multiplication, which I'll write e, and its transpose, contraction, written i, are the creation and annihilation operators of fermions, and this needs no metric at all. The canonical anticommutation relation is just the value of a covector on a vector.

Second: a Clifford generator is what he calls a quasi-particle operator, gamma of v equals e of v plus i of φ v. That is, a creation operator plus an annihilation operator, in the spirit of a Bogoliubov transformation.

Third: the Clifford product lives on the same space as the Grassmann algebra, and is fixed by these generators and associativity. The map φ from vectors to covectors need not be symmetric. It splits into a symmetric part g and an antisymmetric part ω, and the paper suggests that ω might carry physics.

I read the paper together with three later sources. Oziewicz's own sequel, Clifford algebra of multivectors, from 1997, which turns out to answer several of the questions the 1986 paper leaves open. Arkadiusz Jadczyk's 2021 paper On the bundle of Clifford algebras over the space of quadratic forms, which is dedicated to Oziewicz's memory and proves the construction in full. And Garret Sobczyk's recent work building geometric algebras from null vectors, which turns out to contain the 1986 structure almost exactly.

So that you know where we are going, here is the conclusion in one sentence, at the bottom of the slide. What is given without any choice is the evaluation of a covector on a vector. The Grassmann algebra is the Fock space of that evaluation. And a metric is a choice of subspace, the graph of φ. The rest of the talk is about earning that sentence.
