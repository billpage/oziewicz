Why read Oziewicz this way, with derivation notes and verification scripts?

Partly it is a memorial. Zbigniew died in December 2020. He liked to open a talk with slogans, and the three on the left are his. "Science is subjective" and "only a controversial subject is interesting" are from the introduction to his 2009 talk to the Natural Philosophy Alliance. That talk is in the repository, narrated in his own voice, with a transcript. "Everybody has a right to be right" is from the abstract of two papers of 2013, Science must never be objective, and Are peer reviewers Guardians of the Truth? A supplement in the repository gives the place of each slogan and what he argued for it.

The notes try to keep faith with that attitude in three ways. First, each claim is stated in its strongest form before it is tested, and no claim is discounted for being unorthodox. Second, what gets checked is the mathematics, not allegiance: whether an identity holds, whether a construction composes, whether a statement follows from its premises. The notes take no position on which theory nature chooses. Third, the result is reported either way. Where one of his constructions turns out to be textbook relativity in another notation, the note says so. Where a conflict he found survives, the note says that too.

But I want to be honest about where the method parts from him. He held that science needs no criterion for deciding who is right. A repository that checks derivations obviously does not share that view. His own papers assert mathematical results as true or false, so I don't think he held mathematical correctness to be a matter of opinion. But whether he would have accepted this division of labour is an open question, and the repository does not claim that he would.
