Here is the repository at a glance.

The analysis folder holds a numbered derivation ladder. Each step asks one question and answers it with derivations and exact checks. There are four steps so far, and the next slide summarises them.

The supplement folder holds readings that are not ladder steps. There is a reading of relativity in his own terms, which I call bodies first; a summary of an unpublished draft of 2004 on velocity; his views on the practice of science; a review of Tamás Matolcsi's book Spacetime Without Reference Frames; and the newest one, From Grassmann to Clifford, which is the subject of today's talk.

The talks folder has his 2009 talk, What is Special Relativity Theory?, as a narrated slide show, in his own voice and with a synthetic reading of the transcript. The source folder holds verification scripts and figure scripts, and nothing else: no library, no simulation code. The references folder has a link to every paper, and says what was and was not checked in it. His papers themselves are not in the repository. Orientation dot md says what is established, what is open, and what has been corrected or retracted, and there is a glossary and an index of results.

The house rules are on the right. A statement of what a paper says is a reading, in the notes' own words, keyed to the paper's equation numbers; it is not a quotation, and it can be wrong. The word "checked" means that a step was reproduced by computer algebra. It never means that Oziewicz was right, and it never means that the reading is faithful. Each note says what it inherits from earlier notes and what it retracts, and earlier notes are not silently rewritten.

The notes are drafted with Claude, an AI system from Anthropic. I direct the work and review and merge every change, but there has been no external peer review. And I should say plainly that I co-authored one of the papers read here, Concepts of relative velocity, with Zbigniew. That is a relationship, not independence, and you should weigh my readings accordingly.
