This slide is a quick tour of the ladder so far, so that you can see what kind of results the repository produces. It is about relativity, not Clifford algebras.

The ladder starts where Oziewicz ended, with the mass centre, from his paper Many-body mass-centre of 2014 and a longer version of 2017. Step 1 finds that his construction is standard special relativity written without coordinates. His binary velocity is the usual relative velocity, and his mass centre is the centre-of-momentum frame.

Step 2 asks a question beyond what he published. His ternary velocity needs a third observer. Can the mass centre be that third observer? For two bodies, yes, and one of his formulas turns out to be a ternary velocity. For three bodies or more, the mass centre leaves the plane of each pair, and one can show that no Lorentz link can depend on the pair alone, carry one body to the other, and compose. That is the part of what he called the "astonishing conflict" that survives. It is a statement about velocity as an isometry, not about the principle of relativity.

Step 3 asks what remains below the metric, if a body is a process together with a clock. Velocity, its non-reciprocity and the mass centre all survive. His symmetric gamma factor turns out to be the metric itself, and his clock turns out to be a connection, not a time.

Step 4 relates the vorticity of a clock, spin, and the position of the centre. It corrects step 1: a covariant centre worldline does exist, but it needs the angular momentum as well as the mass centre. Each clock picks its own centre, and the spin is the wedge of momentum and angular momentum.

Taken together, no step yet departs from standard relativity or predicts anything new. What is his own is the presentation, several of the results, and above all the questions. Keep that pattern in mind, because the Clifford algebra paper will show it again.
