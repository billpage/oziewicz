Now the quasi-particles. Take a creator and an annihilator together: exterior multiplication by a vector x, plus contraction by a covector α, both acting on multivectors. Equation 22 says that its square is the number α of x.

Here is my reading of what that means. Consider the doubled space: vectors and covectors side by side. On it there is a quadratic form that needs no choice at all: Q of the pair x, α is α of x. It is a neutral form, of signature n, n. Equation 22 says that the map sending the pair x, α to e of x plus i of α is a Clifford map for this form. That is the standard isomorphism between the Clifford algebra of L plus its dual and the algebra of all linear operators on the Grassmann algebra of L.

Now make a choice. Choose any linear map φ from vectors to covectors, and stay on its graph: the pairs v, φ v. On the graph, the operator is gamma of v, equal to e of v plus i of φ v. Its square is φ v evaluated on v, which only sees the symmetric part of φ: it is g of v, v.

So the quadratic form of the Clifford algebra is nothing but evaluation, restricted to the graph of φ. That is the picture on the right. The horizontal axis is the vectors, and it is also the graph of zero: that is where the Grassmann algebra lives. The vertical axis is the covectors. The green line is the graph of φ, with one point, v and φ v, marked on it. The picture is schematic: the doubled space has dimension 2n, and a graph has dimension n.

For completeness: the paper also puts a product on the direct sum of multivectors and forms itself, equations 19 and 20, which it calls a generalized Kähler-Atiyah algebra. That product is not associative, and the paper gives the defect. It is not the product that survives into the later work, so I'll leave it there.
