So, the invitation.

Here is how you can help. First, read an original against its reading. Every note links to the paper it reads, in references slash bibliography dot md, and the readings are keyed to equation numbers so that they can be compared line by line. Second, run a check, or better, break one. Each note has its script in the source folder, and a check that fails is as useful as one that passes. Third, disagree in the open. Open an issue on GitHub. In this repository a disagreement, or a retraction, is recorded the same way as a result. And fourth, suggest what to read next. I have many of Zbigniew's drafts, some of them co-authored. They are not put in the repository, but where one matters, a summary of it can be.

There is also a second project, separate from this repository: an English translation of Zbigniew's calculus book, Calculus without Limits, which he wrote in Spanish. Its repository is private for now, but I'd be glad to invite anyone who would like to help with it.

If you want somewhere to start: Orientation dot md says, briefly, what is established, what is open and what has been retracted. Then the bodies-first supplement, or the 2009 talk in his own voice, for his view of relativity. Then the ladder from step 1.
